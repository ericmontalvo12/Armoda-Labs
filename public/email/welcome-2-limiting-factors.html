Flip over almost any men's supplement and you'll find a "proprietary blend" — a list of ingredients with one combined weight and no individual doses.

Here's why that phrase exists: it's a legal way to put a famous ingredient on the front of the label while including a fraction of the dose used in the research. An ingredient studied at 400mg doesn't do much at 40mg. The blend hides the difference.

This is the single biggest reason people conclude supplements "don't work." The ingredient was fine. The dose was insufficient.

But there's a second thing most brands skip entirely: the difference between total and free testosterone.

Most of the testosterone in your blood is bound to a protein called SHBG. While it's bound, your body can't use it. The free fraction — often just 1–2% of your total — is the only testosterone that actually enters a cell and does something.

This is why two men with the same total T on a lab report can feel completely different. One has more free testosterone available. The other has it locked up.

Any serious formula has to address both problems: real doses you can verify, and ingredients that target the free fraction — not just total. That's the standard we held ourselves to.
