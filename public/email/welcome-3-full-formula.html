Cortisol Support

KSM-66 Ashwagandha — 500 mg

In a 2015 RCT in resistance-trained men, this standardized extract was associated with significantly greater gains in strength and muscle recovery than placebo over 8 weeks, alongside reduced cortisol (Wankhede et al., 2015).

Tongkat Ali — 300 mg

In a 2013 placebo-controlled trial in moderately stressed adults, a standardized extract lowered salivary cortisol and improved the testosterone-to-cortisol ratio alongside better mood scores (Tambi et al., 2012).

Free Testosterone Support

Fenugreek — 500 mg

A 2020 meta-analysis of randomized trials concluded that fenugreek extract had a significant effect on total testosterone, with the proposed mechanism being mild inhibition of the enzymes that convert testosterone to estrogen (Mansoori et al., 2020).

Boron — 9 mg

In a study of healthy men, approximately one week at this dose raised free testosterone and lowered SHBG — the protein that keeps testosterone bound and unusable (Naghii et al., 2011).

Nutrient Foundation

Vitamin D3 — 3,000 IU

In a 12-month RCT, men with low vitamin D who supplemented saw significant increases in total, free, and bioactive testosterone vs. placebo. Most men who work indoors are lower than they think (Pilz et al., 2011).

Zinc — 20 mg

A required cofactor in testosterone production. Research shows that zinc-deficient men have significantly lower testosterone, and supplementation restores levels. Lost through sweat, which puts hard-training men at higher risk (Prasad et al., 1996).

Magnesium — 28.6 mg

Linked to free testosterone through its effect on SHBG. A 2011 trial found magnesium supplementation raised free and total testosterone, with the largest effect in men who trained (Cinar et al., 2011).
